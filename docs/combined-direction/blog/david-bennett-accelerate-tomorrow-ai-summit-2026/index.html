David Bennett at Accelerate Tomorrow AI Summit 2026 in Berlin

David Bennett spoke at Accelerate Tomorrow AI Summit 2026 in Berlin about digital humans, AI avatars and practical human-AI interaction.

By David Bennett18 November 20261 MIN READ

How do digital humans fit into the next wave of enterprise AI?

2–3 June 2026 | Estrel Berlin | Speaker

David Bennett joined the Accelerate Tomorrow AI Summit at Estrel Berlin, where executives, technology leaders and AI practitioners met to discuss how artificial intelligence is moving from experiments into real products and workflows.

His contribution brought a character-production perspective to the discussion. Digital humans combine conversational AI with a visible, expressive interface that can speak, respond and guide an interaction in real time.

Digital humans as an AI interface

Most AI tools are still experienced through text boxes, dashboards or voice assistants. A digital human adds a face, voice and consistent character to the system. This can make complex information easier to present when trust, clarity and social presence matter.

The useful question is not whether an avatar looks impressive. It is whether the character helps someone complete a task, understand information or take part in an experience with less friction.

What production-ready interaction requires

A working digital human needs more than a language model. Facial animation, voice, timing, rendering, memory and conversation rules have to operate as one system. When these layers fall out of sync, the experience quickly feels artificial.

David’s background in visual effects, performance capture and digital character production gives him a practical view of this problem. The goal is a reliable interface that can hold a clear role in training, live events, customer experience or immersive environments.

Why the summit mattered

Accelerate Tomorrow connected technical development with real business use cases. David’s appearance placed digital humans within that wider enterprise AI conversation, alongside questions about implementation, responsibility and measurable value.
